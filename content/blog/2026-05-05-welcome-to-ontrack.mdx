I didn't build OnTrack because I love productivity apps. I've tried most of them. They're great until the day you don't feel like opening one — and that's the only day that actually matters.

I built OnTrack because of two mates who wouldn't let me quit.

## The two-mates principle

I'm a coach. I've worked with hundreds of clients. The single thing that separates the people who get fitter from the people who don't is not the program. It's not the supplement stack. It's not even the gym. It's whether someone else is *expecting them to show up tomorrow*.

Solo discipline is a finite resource. A friend asking "did you train?" at 7pm is infinite. The first time you don't answer, you train tomorrow.

That's what's missing from every habit tracker on the App Store. You can build a 90-day streak in Streaks and your best friend has no idea. The streak only matters to you, which is precisely the problem — *you* are the person it's hardest to be honest with.

OnTrack flips that. Your habits, your sessions, your protocols — they all live inside groups. Your crew can see when you've checked in, when you haven't, and how the streak's going. The accountability isn't a feature. It's the whole point.

## How to build a streak that survives a bad week

Now the practical part. Even with mates watching, you'll have a bad week. Here's the system I use with clients to keep the streak alive when it gets ugly.

**1. Define the streak by minimum, not maximum.**
Most people build streaks around their *good* day. "I'll train 5x a week." Then Monday is busy, Tuesday is sick, Wednesday is a write-off, and by Thursday the streak is dead and you're starting from zero.

Define the streak around your worst day. "I'll do something every day, even if it's 5 minutes of mobility on the kitchen floor." That's a streak you can keep through a flu. The maximum is what you build on top of the minimum, not in place of it.

**2. Make the floor visible.**
Your group needs to see the bad days, not just the good ones. A 5-minute walk logged is a 5-minute walk logged. It says: I'm still here. Hide the bad days and the streak becomes a lie — you can't sustain a lie for 90 days.

**3. Recover, don't reset.**
You'll miss a day. Everyone does. Two rules:
- Never miss two in a row. The second miss is what kills the streak — psychologically more than statistically.
- The day after a miss is the most important day. Show up small. The point is to break the momentum of the miss, not to overcompensate.

**4. Pick mates who text back.**
This is the one nobody tells you. Your accountability group only works if someone in it is actually engaged. One active person is enough. Two is a system. Three is a movement. If your group has gone silent, recruit one new person — don't try to revive the corpse.

**5. Let the data calm you down.**
A bad week feels like the end of the world. Open the app. Look at the last 30 days. The bad week is one bar in a chart of mostly-green bars. Zoom out and the panic deflates.

## The honest pitch

OnTrack isn't magic. It won't make you motivated. Nothing will, reliably. What it does is make consistency social — your effort gets witnessed, your slips get noticed, and the streak becomes something you're not the only one invested in.

If that's the kind of thing you've been looking for, [download it](/download). If it's not, that's fine too — but the principle still works. Find two mates. Tell them what you're doing. Show up tomorrow.

That's the whole game.
